# Teams, the schedule, and notices

This file explains how RIM's teams work on the website: a team's own space, what team leads can do, the Scheduler, Notifications and the emails people get, joining a session on Zoom, and signing in. It describes the site as it is now. The address of the live site is rim-next.vercel.app (the old site at rootedinmindfulness.org is still separate until the launch).

A note on words. People read "team" everywhere on the site. Some addresses still contain the older word "hub" (for example /account/hub/greeter); that is only the address, and it means the same thing. "Lead" and "coordinator" mean the same person: the site marks them **Coordinator**.

## Signing in

There are no passwords. A person signs in with a 6-digit code sent by email.

- **Where:** on a phone, Menu, then Sign in; on a computer, Members at the top of the page, then Sign in. The sign-in page is rim-next.vercel.app/login.
- **How:** type the email address RIM has for you and choose **Send code**. An email from Rooted In Mindfulness arrives with a 6-digit code. Type the six digits and choose **Sign in**. The email also has a button, **Sign me in from this device**, which opens the code page with the code already filled in; the person still taps Sign in.
- **The code works for 30 minutes.** Several codes can be valid at once, so asking for a new one never breaks anything. A person usually stays signed in on that phone or computer for a long time (about three months), so they won't need a code every visit.
- **The first time,** a Welcome page asks for first and last name and shows the Community Care Agreements, with a box to tick. Then the person arrives at My Home with the note "You're all set. Welcome to RIM."
- **The first sign-in matters for volunteers.** A person added to a team before they ever signed in gets none of the team's emails until they sign in once. Leads can see who hasn't (see "What leads can do").
- **If the code doesn't come:** look in spam or junk; choose **send a new code** below the code boxes; or **Start over** if the address was typed wrong.
- **"We don't have an account for" that address:** the person may be using a different email from the one RIM has. They can try their other address or write to support@rootedinmindfulness.org. **Become a member** is only for people new to RIM.
- **"Your sign-in email has changed":** if someone types an address their account used to have, the page says so, shows the new address partly hidden (the first letter and the domain), and offers one button, **Send code to my new email**. The way out reads **← Back to sign in**.
- **"We could not sign you in":** the code was wrong, had expired, or there were several tries in a short time. Wait a few minutes, choose Try again, and ask for a new code.
- **Signing out** is at the foot of the menu column (My Profile and Sign out sit together).

## Finding your team

- **My Teams** (rim-next.vercel.app/account/teams) lists the teams a person is on. It appears in the personal menu only for people who are on a team. Each team shows **Coordinator** if the person leads it. Someone with no team who reaches the page sees what teams are and a **Volunteer with RIM** button.
- Choosing a team opens its space. Inside a team, the left column (on a phone, the ☰ beside the team's name) belongs to that team. It lists Home, the team's apps if it has any, Updates, Conversations, Files and Members, and at its foot My Profile and Sign out. A way back to My Teams is there too.
- Every page keeps the RIM website's own header at the top, so a person can always get back to the main site and to My Home.

## A team's space

Every team's space has the same parts, in the same places, whatever the team does.

### Home

A team's Home is short on purpose. In order:

1. **The team's name.**
2. **The team's line:** one sentence about the team, written by its leads. If they haven't written one yet, it reads "Your team's shared space."
3. **The team's tool,** if it has one, in a single card: the tool's name, one line about it, and one button (for example **Open the Scheduler**). For a scheduling team, the line is a true count, such as "3 sessions in the next two weeks need a greeter", or "Every session in the next two weeks has a greeter", or "No sessions on the schedule in the next two weeks."
4. **Anything waiting for this person,** if there is something ("N items need your attention").
5. **The places:** Conversations, Files, Members, any other tools, and any links the team keeps.

Home doesn't grow with every message or file. The news lives in Updates; the discussions live in Conversations.

### Updates

Updates is the team's news, read like an inbox. Newest first, grouped under day headings.

- **Needs you** sits at the top: the team's sessions in the next two weeks that still need someone. Each has its action right there, in the team's words: **Take this session**, **Sign up to greet**, or **I'll cover this** (for a teammate's cover request). A session leaves Needs you once someone takes it. A lead who can't take sessions themselves sees them with a link instead.
- **The filters** are pills along the top: **All**, **For you**, and **Unread**, each with a count. Beside them, **View** picks a kind: Everything, Schedule, Conversations, Files, or Members, each with its unread count.
- **Every line opens the exact thing:** the session in the Scheduler, the reply in a conversation, the comment on a file, the person on Members.
- **Read marks:** opening an item marks it read. **Mark all as read** clears the dots. Nothing ever leaves the list; Everything always shows everything.
- The number on Updates in the left column counts only unread items that are for this person.
- **What the schedule history shows:** who took a session, who was put on one, rotations set and ended, and cover requests with where they stand (Open, Covered by someone, Closed, Passed). When a session is left with no one, the team sees "Program · date needs a greeter" (in the team's words) without naming anyone.
- **What only leads see:** when someone drops a session without asking for cover, is taken off one, comes off a rotation, or leaves the team, only the team's leads and the person themselves see that line. The team sees the need, not the person. (Asking for cover is different: the team is being asked, so the person asking is named to everyone.)

### Conversations

Conversations are the team's discussions, one topic each.

- **+ New topic**, a title and a message, then **Post topic**. The team's coordinators are told about every new topic automatically. Under **Notify team members**, the writer can tick anyone else who should be told.
- **Replies:** write in the box at the bottom and choose **Post reply**. Everyone following the topic is told.
- **Follow** beside a title means "tell me about every reply". It changes to **Following**; tapping again stops it.
- **Filters:** Active and Archived, and View by category. Pinned topics lead the list.
- **Removing a conversation** takes two steps: it is archived first, and only an archived conversation can be deleted. A deleted conversation goes to the team's Trash, where a lead can restore it.

### Files

Files holds the team's shared documents. The documents live in Google Drive, but **nobody needs a Google account**: RIM opens them for the person.

- **Opening a file** opens its own page on RIM, with the document shown right there. To change it, choose **Edit in Google Docs**; it opens in Google. In Google, a person's edits may show as "Anonymous" with an animal's name. That is normal and expected.
- **Comments:** below a file, anyone on the team can leave a comment for the team. When posting, the writer chooses who is told (no one, everyone, or chosen people). Telling people is picked fresh each time; nobody is signed up to every file.
- **Drafts:** a document created through RIM starts as the creator's private draft, shown under "Your drafts", until they choose to share it with the team. Uploads and folders are shared at once.
- **"Created by"** is RIM's own record of who added a file. A file added directly in Google Drive reads "Added directly in Google Drive."
- **Search file names** finds files in the folder being looked at (not across the whole site). Favorites, colours and the sort order are personal: they change only what this person sees. A pinned file is shared with the team and shows who pinned it.
- **Removing a file** never deletes it at once. **Request removal** sends a request to the team's leads; the file waits under **Pending removal**. A lead chooses **Approve removal** (the file goes to Google's trash, recoverable for 30 days by writing to support) or **Keep** (it stays).
- **Leads only.** Every team has two areas: **Team files**, for everyone on the team, and **Leads only**, for the team's leads. Members see Team files only. Leads switch between the two and can move a file or a whole folder either way from its ⋯ menu (**Move to Team files**, **Move to Leads only**). Moving something into Leads only also cuts off any links to it that were handed out before. News about Leads only files reaches only the leads.

### Members

Members lists everyone on the team, with the **Coordinators** at the top: the people to ask.

What members see: names, and who the coordinators are. What only leads see: everyone's email address, private coordinator notes, and **"Hasn't signed in yet."** beside anyone who has never signed in to the new site ("Team emails start when they do").

### Trash

Trash appears at the foot of the team's column only for leads (and the guiding teacher and admins). It holds deleted conversations. Each has **Restore** (puts it back) and **Delete permanently** (cannot be undone).

## What leads can do that members can't

A lead is an **Active** coordinator of the team. Jesse, as guiding teacher, can act as a lead on every team. A coordinator who is Paused or Inactive has no lead powers until set back to Active.

Leads can:

- **Write the team's line** on Home (**Write this team's line** or **Edit this line**, then Save).
- **See who hasn't signed in yet** on Members, and everyone's email address.
- **Add someone** with **+ Add Member** (type at least three letters of a name or email). Someone who has never signed in to the new site won't appear in that list; an admin or the registrar adds them from the Member Registry instead.
- **Change a member's settings** with **Edit** beside a name: their **Status** (Active, Paused, Inactive), making them a **Coordinator**, **Can take sessions on the schedule**, **Receives team notifications**, and a private **Coordinator note**.
- **Pause someone** (a break). If they have sessions coming, the lead is asked: **Keep their sessions**, or **Take them off their sessions** so the team can take them. A paused person who keeps their sessions still gets their reminders. On the Hosting Team, a paused host who keeps their sessions can't open the room as host.
- **Set someone Inactive** (they have left the team). Their upcoming sessions on that team open, their rotations end, they are told, and the team hears about any open session in the next two weeks. Setting them back to **Active** clears their pause note.
- **Manage Files' Leads only area,** move things between areas, and approve or keep removal requests.
- **Restore or permanently delete** conversations in Trash, and moderate conversations.
- **Run the team's schedule** (see "What leads do in the Scheduler").
- **Get the lead's brief** by email (see "Emails about the schedule").
- **Be told about every new conversation** on the team.

Things leads can't do: change the team's settings (its name, address, which tools it has, whether its schedule is open sign-up). Only an admin does that, from Team settings.

## The Scheduler

The Scheduler (rim-next.vercel.app/tools/schedule, opened from a team's Home with **Open the Scheduler**) is where a team sees which sessions it serves and who is on each one. Each scheduling team sees only its own programs and its own sessions. The teams that use it today are the Hosting Team, Peer-Led Silent Meditation, Greeter, and Audio/Visual.

Only the program's **hosting team** hosts the session and opens the online room. A greeter or AV volunteer serves in person and never gets a host code or "Enter room". Peer-Led Silent Meditation guides are both host and facilitator for Good Morning and Good Evening.

### Reading the schedule

- Each row is one session: its day and time on the left, then who is serving it.
- The pills: **All** (every session), **Needs help** (the ones that still need someone; it turns amber for leads when there are some), and **My schedule** (yours). The small arrow beside My schedule picks a teammate to see their sessions; the list shows the whole team and scrolls.
- **"via rotation"** on a session means it comes from a regular turn the leads set up.
- A link from an email or from Updates opens the Scheduler at that exact session, marked.

### Two kinds of team

- **One person per session** (Hosting Team, Peer-Led Silent Meditation, Audio/Visual). A session either has its person or needs one ("Host needed", in the team's words).
- **Open sign-up** (Greeter). Several people can share one session. There are no cover requests; people sign up and cancel their own sign-up.

### Taking a session

- One person per session: on a session that needs someone, choose **Yes, I can host this** (in the team's words), check the day and time, then **Yes, I'll host this**. The session is theirs and a confirmation email is sent.
- Open sign-up: **I'll be the first** where no one has signed up yet, or **I'll be there too**. One tap signs them up, the row shows "you" beside their name, and a confirmation email is sent.
- Updates' Needs you offers the same action in place.

### Can't make it: asking for cover

- On a one-person team, a person who can't make their session chooses **Ask the team to cover**, adds a note if they like, then **Send to the team**. Teammates who can take sessions are emailed ("Cover needed"). **The session stays theirs until someone steps in.**
- **Changed plans again?** **Cancel my request**, then **Yes, cancel the request**. The session stays theirs.
- **Stepping in for a teammate:** on their session, **Yes, I can cover**, then **Yes, I'll cover this**. The person who asked gets "Your session is covered"; the one stepping in gets a confirmation.
- On the Greeter team, a person cancels with **Cancel my signup**. If they are the only greeter for that session, they are asked first ("If you cancel, we'll let the team know it needs a greeter"), and if the session is within two weeks the team hears.
- After any action, a notice at the bottom of the screen says what happened, including whether the team was told. It stays until closed.

### Rotations (regular turns)

A rotation fills sessions automatically from a pattern, so nobody has to sign up week by week. Leads set them up on the **Rotations** tab. The patterns:

- **Same every week.**
- **Every other week:** two people take turns week by week from a chosen first date, whatever the calendar month does. Only for programs that meet every week.
- **Alternate by week of the month:** for example one person on the 1st and 3rd Thursday, another on the 2nd and 4th. A 5th-week person is optional.
- **Custom.**

Rotation sessions are filled a few weeks ahead. Next month's sessions appear around the 17th of this month, and the person gets a "You're scheduled" email then. Someone who took a session themselves, or has a cover request, is kept unless the lead chooses otherwise. If a program stops meeting every week, an every-other-week pair ends together.

### What leads do in the Scheduler

Leads (and Jesse, and the Scheduling manager role) have full say over their own team's schedule:

- **Assign someone…** to an open session (the person is emailed).
- **Manage coverage** on a session someone has: **Ask the team to cover** on their behalf, **Remove** them (asked first: **Yes, remove** or **Keep them on**), or **Reassign to me**.
- **Clear request** when someone asked for cover but will make it after all (they keep the session).
- On the Greeter team: **Remove** beside a person's name.
- **Rotations:** **Set up** or **Edit** a day's rotation, choose the pattern and people, check the **Preview**, then **Save & apply**. A note then says how many sessions it filled and who has the next ones. If someone is already on a date, the lead chooses **Leave them alone**, **Replace all**, or **Decide one by one**.
- **Change or end a rotation** with **Reset** (the day's name): **Remove from rotation** (one person), **Set end date** (keeps sessions up to a date), or reset the whole day. Everyone affected is emailed.
- When a lead takes someone off a session within two weeks, the team hears that the session needs someone. When a lead reshapes the schedule (ending or resetting rotations), the team is not emailed straight away; the Monday look-ahead and the lead's brief cover it.

## Emails about the schedule: who is told what, and when

- **Confirmations** name the role, the team and the time, for example "You're greeting: [program] · [date and time]". The same for being taken off ("You're no longer hosting") and "Your session is covered".
- **When a session close at hand loses its last person:** if a session within the next two weeks is left with no one on a team (someone cancels, is taken off, is paused or set Inactive, leaves the team), that team's members who can take sessions get one email at once with a button to take it. The person who made the change and the person taken off are not emailed about it. Further out, the Monday look-ahead covers it.
- **Every Monday,** each team's members get the next two weeks' sessions that still need someone.
- **The lead's brief** replaces that Monday list for the team's coordinators: one email laid out for decisions. It lists each open session, why it's open, and who on the team can take it, plus what changed since last Monday (people stepping back included) and how many sessions are set. On other days it comes only when a session two days away still needs someone.
- **Two days before** each session, every person serving it gets a reminder with the time, their role, and everyone serving that session ("Host: …", "AV: none yet", "Greeters: …").
- **Rotation emails:** "You're scheduled for [program]", "You're no longer scheduled for [program] on [date]", "You've been removed from the [program] rotation", "Your [role] rotation has ended".
- **Schedule changes:** if a program stops meeting on some days, or moves to another team, the people whose sessions no longer happen are told.
- A host is not emailed about a greeter or AV gap; the Scheduler and their reminder show it.

## Notifications and the email choice

**Notifications** (rim-next.vercel.app/account/notifications, under My Home in the personal menu, with an unread count) is one place for everything RIM tells a person: their sessions, registrations, receipts, account, team conversations, files, and sessions that need someone. It works for people who aren't on any team too. My Home shows one line ("You have 3 new notifications") when there's news.

- Each row is one line: where it's from, what happened, and when, for example "Hosting Team · Schedule". Tapping it opens the details and one button that goes there ("Open the conversation", "Open the schedule"). Opening it marks it read. Several notices about the same thing on the same day fold together ("2 more").
- **Filters:** **All** and **Unread**; **From** (one team; every team the person is on is listed); **View** (one kind: Schedule, Conversations, Files, Programs, or Your account). Each shows its unread count. **Mark all as read** marks only what is showing.
- **Needs you** gathers, from every team the person is on, the sessions in the next two weeks they could take, with the Scheduler's own button.
- Most emails about a person or their teams also appear in Notifications. A few don't, such as the sign-in code, the new-member welcome and the daily summary.

**The email choice** (My Profile, under Notifications, for people on a team) decides how **team news** is emailed: **Email me each one** (the usual), **One email a day** (a morning summary, "What's new on your teams"), or **Don't email me team news**. Then **Save email choice**. Team news means conversations, files, and sessions the team needs. Whatever is chosen:

- Emails **about the person** always come: their own sessions, rotations, registrations, receipts and account.
- **Urgent team news** always comes: a session within two days that needs someone, or a cover request for one.
- If a team's lead has switched off **Receives team notifications** for someone, that person gets team news only in Notifications, not by email, urgent or not.

## Joining a session on Zoom

RIM runs its online sessions on Zoom. RIM arranges the meeting; Zoom is the room. **Nobody signs in to Zoom** and nobody needs a Zoom account.

### The door

Every Join button (on My Home, in the Scheduler, in emails) goes through one RIM page, the door, at an address like rim-next.vercel.app/session/[program]/enter. The door checks, in order: that the person is signed in (or has a guest link), that the program is still running, that it is an online or hybrid program, that it is the right time, and that the person may join (see below). Then it sends them into Zoom.

**When it opens:** on My Home, members see **Join on Zoom** from **10 minutes before** the start; the door itself lets people in from 30 minutes before. The hosting team can open the room from **30 minutes before** (My Home shows **Enter Zoom as host**; the Scheduler shows **Enter room**). The door stays open until **30 minutes after** the end.

**Who may join:** a program with **Open entry** turned on admits anyone signed in. A program that takes registrations admits people registered for it (not people on the waiting list), plus the hosting team, the program's teachers, and Jesse. Someone with the program's **guest link** can come in without an account, during the same window.

**When it can't let someone in,** the door shows a plain page, never a silent bounce: the room isn't open yet (and when it opens, in Central Time), the program has ended, the person isn't registered, or Zoom is busy. Each has one way forward: **Try again**, **Back to My Home**, or **See the program page**.

### Hosts claiming host

- The host opens the room from My Home. Their page says **You're hosting today** and shows a 6-digit **host code**. They keep it in view and choose **Join as host**.
- The first time Zoom opens on a phone or computer, it asks for a name: first name and last initial. Zoom remembers it.
- In Zoom: **Participants**, then **Claim Host** (on a phone it may be under the three dots), type the code, confirm. The host controls are theirs.
- **The code is for the team.** Members never see it.
- **The backup:** another member of the hosting team opening the room sees who is hosting today and the same code. If the host is there, there is nothing to do. If not, the backup claims host so the session is never left without one. Only one person holds host at a time; the host can make others co-hosts inside Zoom.
- The program's teacher sees "You're teaching" with the code, as a backup.

### Outside rooms

A few programs meet in someone else's online room (another organization's Zoom, or Google Meet). For these, RIM's door still checks everything above, then forwards the person to that room. There is no RIM host code and no recording. The button names the service, for example "Join on Google Meet". A program can also allow anyone to join from its public program page, still through the door and within the time window, so the outside link itself is never shown.

## Helping someone who is stuck

- Most questions about leading a team go to the team's coordinators (marked Coordinator on Members), then to support@rootedinmindfulness.org or Jesse.
- The tutorials at rim-next.vercel.app/tutorials walk through all of this with drawings, on a phone or a computer.
