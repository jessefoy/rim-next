When borrowing a structural device from a rich reference (e.g. the Esther Perel site — uppercase section eyebrows, full-bleed color bands), the scaffolding works there only because it sits inside a rich composition: illustrations, confident color, scale contrast, asymmetry. **Transplanted into a sparse reading column, the same device reads as cheap, not minimal** — a small gray uppercase label becomes a *form label*; a beige-on-beige centered-text band becomes *newsletter furniture*. (Session 148: chapter eyebrows + a closing band were built and reverted for exactly this — "didn't feel well designed aesthetically.")

**Why:** minimalism is *restraint within a considered whole*; sparseness is *missing substance*. They look alike in a wireframe and opposite when rendered. Copying the bones of a design without its flesh produces the second, not the first.

**How to apply:** before adding a reference's structural device, ask "do we have the *substance* that justifies it here — real visual anchors, composition, color confidence — or just the empty frame?" If only the frame, bring the substance first or don't add the device. Pairs with [[feedback-honor-the-reference]] and the RIM [[design-principles]] (restraint over addition).
