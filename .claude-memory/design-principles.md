## The Foundation

Every design decision at RIM derives from one Dharma principle: **clear seeing is the prerequisite for wise and compassionate response.**

This is not aesthetic preference. It is the ground from which right action becomes possible. When the mind is cluttered, it cannot see clearly. When it cannot see clearly, it cannot respond with wisdom. It can only react. This applies to every screen — public, member, volunteer, admin.

**Every screen should ask: does this create the conditions for clear seeing?**

## The Principles

**Restraint over addition.** The question is never "what can we add?" — it is "what can we remove?" Every element on a page earns its presence. If it doesn't communicate something the visitor needs right now, it is friction. It competes for attention. It dilutes the signal.

**Content is the point.** The design exists to serve it — not to organize it, package it, or brand it into submission. When content needs emphasis, use typographic weight and scale — not color, animation, or icons.

**Forms as thresholds.** Registration and sign-in are not administrative tasks — they are moments of transition from visitor to participant. Forms should be calm, unhurried, plain-language, and feel like being welcomed — not processed.

**Design for overwhelmed users.** Many RIM members experience tech anxiety. A confusing screen triggers reactive tapping, not careful reading. Rules: remove ruthlessly, make the primary action visually dominant, make random tapping survivable, use plain-language state headers (sentences, not labels), make escape paths visible and named.

**The Dharma does not stop at the threshold of an admin interface.** It is either present in the whole or it isn't present at all. Volunteer tools and admin screens receive the same design care as the public site.

**One dominant action per state.** Never two buttons of similar visual weight competing for attention. The thing they should do next should be so visually obvious it draws the eye even when the brain is offline.

## The Aesthetic

Warm, grounded, unhurried. Cool greys (#f5f5f5), muted teals (#135274), nothing stark, nothing cold. Quincy-cf serif for headings, Open Sans for body. Generous line height (1.7), constrained reading column (700px). No drop shadows, no floating cards, no notification badges competing for attention. Separation through color and whitespace — not borders and shadows.

## How to Apply

Before writing any CSS or building any UI component:
1. Can anything be removed from this screen?
2. Is there one clear primary action?
3. Would this work for someone overwhelmed and not reading carefully?
4. Does it feel calm, or does it feel busy?
5. Am I adding something because it's needed, or because "apps usually have this"?
