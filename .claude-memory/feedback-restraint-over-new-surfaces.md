# Restraint over reactive new surfaces

When a user brings a list of pain points, the instinct is to build something new for them — a new view, a new page, a new tab. Resist it. The first move is to make the surfaces that already exist **trustworthy and clear**, not to add another one.

## The session-141 trigger

Maria (host coordinator) gave detailed Scheduler feedback ending with a structural complaint: the tool felt *"disjointed … too many discrete little pages you connect in your own head."* The reflex — mine — was to design a new all-in-one **Coverage grid** (programs × weeks). I designed it, Jesse approved a slice, I built it. Then it broke twice under real data: a flat, unusable dump on mobile, and it fragmented multi-day programs / retreats (it silently assumed one-weekday-weekly). **We reverted it.** The real wins were polishing what already existed — the time-ordered agenda (which handles every program shape) made gap-aware, and the rotation editor made to confirm its result in place.

Jesse named the lesson directly: *"I feel like maybe we're being reactive to her feedback … I start to get uncomfortable with too many things."*

## The rule

- A list of complaints is **not** a spec for a new surface. Map each complaint to the existing surface that should get better, and fix those first.
- Reach for a **new** view/page/tab only when no existing surface can hold the need — and hold it to a higher bar than a fix: it must handle **every real data shape** (at RIM: single-day, multi-day, recurring, retreats/events), work on **mobile**, and not **fragment** the picture. If it can't, it's a worse answer than improving the existing surface, even if it demos well on desktop.
- Restraint applies to our own process, not just the UI. "What can we remove / not add?" is the RIM design question pointed at the build plan.

## How this connects

- **feedback-pivot-when-fragile** — the grid hit two structural failures (mobile, multi-day); that's the signal to stop patching and step back. Here, stepping back meant *not building it at all*.
- **design-principles** — restraint, calm, one dominant action; fewer well-made surfaces serve overwhelmed users better than more surfaces.
- The healthy version still honors the feedback: we addressed Maria's structural complaint by making the agenda + rotation editor dependable, told her plainly we'd chosen not to add a page, and offered to design a bigger view *with* her if the need persisted.
