When Jesse says a design makes him uneasy, don't iterate on the model he's uneasy with. Go back to first principles: who the people are, what each one actually comes to do, and what's already true in the organization. Then propose a fresh shape.

**Why:** On 2026-10-23 the Registration design had become a permission system (roster sharing levels, per-question audiences). Every piece was defensible, but Jesse said: *"I don't know. I'm actually a little uncomfortable with this. I remember, with a feature in Bluebird DAW… we decided to go back and think about it freshly, and we came up with a really awesome feature for the drums. I would like you to do the same."* The fresh idea, "Who's helping, who's asking" (every question is asked by a helper; each helper gets one page that is plainly theirs), replaced the whole permission layer. He liked it at once: *"I like that. I think that's good."*

**How to apply:** Name what felt wrong in plain words (here: settings with no person behind them). Look for facts already true at RIM that make the machinery unnecessary. Present the new idea with the mockup updated to match, and list what it replaces. Related: [[feedback-pivot-when-fragile]], [[feedback-restraint-over-new-surfaces]].
