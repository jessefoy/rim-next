# Write the words in Programs, send from Registration

Established registration products keep a program's own words (the confirmation note, the "what to bring" reminder text, and when the reminder goes out) on the program's setup screen, and they keep every act of sending to people, plus the record of what went to whom, with the people who run the registrations. RIM should split the same way: Programs authors the messages, Registration sends, resends and remembers them. Rock RMS, the closest analogue to RIM, stores "confirmation details", "reminder details" and the reminder date on each dated run of an event, while Planning Center, Rock, Retreat Guru and Eventbrite put resends, balance-due nudges and ad hoc messages in the registrant list, and Eventbrite even makes "email attendees" a separate permission from editing the event. Across more than twenty products, registration features fall into a dozen families, and RIM already covers the core well: capacity with an automatic waitlist, voluntary dana with a way to give nothing, prefilled member details, calendar links, and a Zoom door that admits only registrants. (Correction, 2026-10-23: an earlier draft said RIM's reminder also reaches people who register late. A check of the code found it doesn't: the daily run only looks back 24 hours, so a late registrant gets the reminder only when a registrar sends it by hand. The build plan makes it true.) RIM's real gaps are in memory and care, not in selling: no history, unsigned notes, no way to register someone who phones, no word to a person the registrar cancels, waitlist offers that never expire, no attendance, and no safe way to share a roster. Jesse's per-field audience has exactly one market precedent (Retreat Guru's per-question role permissions), and no product found tells the registrant, at the question, who will read the answer, so RIM can lead here; it first needs answers tied to a stable question rather than to the question's wording. Build memory and privacy first, then the tools for running a program well, and leave out fees, scarcity pricing and scramble-style waitlists, which work against dana and care.

## Every product studied keeps per-program message content on the setup screen

The dominant pattern has two layers. A **site-wide layer** holds the fixed system wording and the logic (order details, receipts, waitlist handling), and a **per-event layer** holds a free-text message that the person setting up the event writes next to the event's other settings. Planning Center writes the custom confirmation message on the signup's own Configuration tab, with up to five attachments and an optional extra message per selection type, and the email wraps it with the registration details, a calendar link and a link back to the signup ([Planning Center: custom confirmation message](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139350-create-a-custom-confirmation-message.html)). Its scheduled reminders also sit under the signup's Configuration, set from one hour to four weeks before the event ([Planning Center: send emails](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139345-send-emails-to-registration-contacts-and-attendees.html)). Realm puts "Customize Confirmation Email" under the event's own advanced options and its reminders (one to six days, or one or two weeks, before) on the event too ([Realm: confirmation email](https://help.acst.com/en/realm/how-to/registration-events/customize-the-event-confirmation-email); [Realm: reminders](https://help.acst.com/en/realm/how-to/registration-events/send-reminders-before-an-event)). CiviCRM lets a reminder be set on one event's Schedule Reminders tab, and "any reminder set up from the event configuration screen applies only to that specific event" ([CiviCRM: scheduled reminders](https://docs.civicrm.org/user/en/latest/email/scheduled-reminders/)). Eventbrite and Humanitix also keep the confirmation message in the event's own settings, though both of those findings come from search summaries rather than pages read in full ([Eventbrite help 3281](https://www.eventbrite.com/support/articleredirect?anum=3281); [Humanitix: confirmation email](https://help.humanitix.com/en/articles/9938796-do-ticket-buyers-receive-a-confirmation-email-for-their-purchase)).

**Rock RMS is the closest match to RIM and to the agreed two-app design.** Rock separates a reusable Registration Template, which holds the confirmation, reminder and payment-reminder email bodies, from each dated Instance, which holds its own "Confirmation details" text, its own "Reminder details" text (for things like what to bring), a contact person, and the date and optional time its reminder goes out ([Rock U: Registration Instances](https://chiptraining.rockrms.com/ask-chip-training/rocku/137)). Rock's trainer says most churches leave the template bodies "pretty stock" because they carry logic such as remaining balances and waitlist handling ([Rock U: Registration Templates](https://chiptraining.rockrms.com/ask-chip-training/rocku/150)). That is RIM today: the site-wide template manager owns the logic-bearing wording, and the program supplies a plain message. Both of these Rock sources are training-video transcripts, which are reliable for what the product does but not formal documentation.

**Sending, by contrast, lives with the registrations.** Planning Center's per-registration Email menu offers "Resend confirmation emails", "Incomplete attendee info reminder" and "Balance due reminder", and its bulk sends start from the Registrations side ([Planning Center: send emails](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139345-send-emails-to-registration-contacts-and-attendees.html)). Rock resends confirmations from Registration Details and messages the registrars of selected people from the Registrants tab ([Rock: Manage Event Registrations](https://community.rockrms.com/documentation/church-management/event-calendar/event-registrations/manage-event-registrations)). Retreat Guru lets staff email one participant from the edit-registration page using a template ([Retreat Guru: email templates](https://help.retreat.guru/knowledge/email-templates)). Eventbrite reviews scheduled, queued and sent emails with their recipients under Manage attendees, and requires a separate permission to "email attendees" ([Eventbrite help 3327](https://www.eventbrite.com/support/articleredirect?anum=3327)). The honest counterexample is that Eventbrite also lets organizers edit the automatic reminder's text from that attendee area (a search summary, [Eventbrite help 3310](https://www.eventbrite.co.uk/support/articleredirect?anum=3310)), and Tito and Humanitix send no automatic reminder at all, so their "reminders" are messages an organizer schedules from the attendee side ([Tito: remind attendees](https://help.tito.io/en/articles/2038102-how-do-i-remind-attendees-to-complete-their-tickets); [Humanitix: reminder email](https://help.humanitix.com/en/articles/8906262-do-ticket-buyers-automatically-get-an-event-reminder-email), both search summaries). Where a product has a real automatic reminder tied to the event, its content lives with the event.

**The decision for RIM:** Programs owns the confirmation message, the reminder message, the reminder's timing rule, and (when built) pre-arrival and follow-up text, because these describe the program and are written once by the person who knows it. Registration owns everything that touches a person: resend, remind one or the rest, message all or a filtered group, waitlist offers, dana nudges, cancellation notices, and a per-person record of what was sent and when. Registration should show the program's upcoming automatic sends read-only, with a link back to edit them in Programs, so the registrar can see what is about to go out without the words having two homes. The ability to message registrants should be its own registration-team power, as Eventbrite makes it.

Two refinements follow from the evidence. First, **relative timing is the majority pattern**: Eventbrite (48 hours by default), Wild Apricot ("N days before"), Planning Center and Realm all count back from the start, and Eventbrite gives online events three reminders, at 48 hours, 2 hours and 10 minutes ([Eventbrite: automatic emails](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/748624/); [Wild Apricot: event emails](https://gethelp.wildapricot.com/en/articles/31-event-emails), search summary). RIM's fixed Reminder Date at 9 AM matches Rock, but it must be filled for every run; "N days before the first session" with an optional fixed-date override removes that chore, and a short "starting soon" note with the room link suits Zoom programs. RIM's rule of sending to every confirmed person "who hasn't had it" is the right intent, but today the daily run only looks back 24 hours, so it doesn't reach people who register after the reminder went out (corrected 2026-10-23 against the code). Build it so it does: CiviCRM's start-relative reminders and Eventbrite's "people who are registered at the time it's sent" ([Eventbrite help 3327](https://www.eventbrite.com/support/articleredirect?anum=3327)) show the difference. Second, **no product re-sends an edited message automatically**. Eventbrite alone sends an automatic notice when date, time or place changes, and once an email is queued it "can't [be] cancel[led] or edit[ed]" ([Eventbrite: automatic emails](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/748624/); [Eventbrite help 3327](https://www.eventbrite.com/support/articleredirect?anum=3327)). Retreat Guru shows the drift risk of the alternative model: templates inserted into a program are copied, and "later changes to the original template do not carry over" ([Retreat Guru: email templates](https://help.retreat.guru/knowledge/email-templates)). When a program's time, place, link or messages change after people have registered, the Programs editor should say so and offer "send an update to the N people registered", with that send recorded in Registration.

## Rosters travel on a need-to-know basis, and the door sees least

The products converge on three ideas: **a small set of named roles down to "door only" and "read only"; roles granted per event or series, not only account-wide; and the riskiest powers (export, refunds, finances, sharing access onward) kept above day-to-day roster work.** Tito's six roles are the clearest model. Staff do "day-to-day work: orders, attendees, check-in, badge printing, and messages" but cannot refund, export data or change settings; Door is check-in only; Viewer reads and changes nothing; each role can be scoped to named events or series, and a narrowed role "can only ever hold less than the role it came from" ([Tito: Team](https://go.tito.io/docs/account/team)). Humanitix separates "Manage orders, attendees & email campaigns" (helpers who "cannot change events or see sensitive sales data") from finances and from scanning-only access, which shows the event only in the app, and turns re-sharing off by default ([Humanitix: share event access](https://help.humanitix.com/en/articles/8892751-how-to-share-event-access-with-another-user-or-team)). Planning Center adds Managers per signup, who see contact details "only [for] attendees in signup", and a Counter permission that lets an usher record headcounts without any access to the admin side ([Planning Center: permissions in Registrations](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/136860-permissions-in-registrations.html); [Planning Center: permissions in Check-Ins](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/permissions-in-check-ins.html)).

RIM's agreed share levels map cleanly onto this. **Count only** is Planning Center's headcounter; **names** is Tito Door or Eventbrite's check-in role; **names and contact** is Tito Staff or Humanitix's attendee helper. The closest peer is a Buddhist retreat centre, Dhanakosa, whose office shares with retreat teams only what it "consider[s] relevant to the retreat teams' job", typically retreat lists and "health and support needs", while teams "do not have access to the Dhanakosa database or personal contact details" and contact with retreatants goes through the office ([Dhanakosa: privacy for retreat teams, 2025](https://www.dhanakosa.com/system/files/resources/Managing%20privacy%20and%20GDPR%20for%20retreat%20teams%202025.pdf)). That supports defaulting teachers and teams to names, with contact routed through the registrar unless there is a reason to give more. The warning sign is Eventbrite, whose "Basic reports" permission includes "Custom questions responses": answers travel with a whole report, with no way to hold some back ([Eventbrite: permissions definitions](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/362073/)).

### Who may read each answer: guidance for Jesse's per-field audience

**Only one product lets an organizer restrict individual answers by role.** Retreat Guru's "Registration Data Permissions" let admins "control which members of your team can access guest responses to certain registration questions", with role permissions set on each field (its example shows Birth Date visible only to the Registrar role), and admin-only field sets such as "HKP Notes" for housekeeping ([Retreat Guru: features](https://software.retreat.guru/features), vendor marketing; [Retreat Guru: forms and fields](https://help.retreat.guru/knowledge/forms)). Tito, Humanitix, Eventbrite and Planning Center gate answers by whole-report or whole-role permission, and Planning Center's permissions page does not even say whether managers see form answers. **No product or centre found tells the registrant, at the question, who will read the answer**, and no meditation centre publicly says who sees health or trauma disclosures, though Deer Park asks about "any psychiatric or psychological diagnoses and mental health medications" (search summary, [Deer Park registration](https://deerparkmonastery.org/retreats/clarity-hamlet/119727/register)). RIM's requirement is ahead of the market, which means the defaults have to come from privacy guidance rather than from copying a product.

The guidance points to six rules. **Default every new question to the Registration team only**, so an answer is never shared by accident; this follows the FTC's need-to-know principle and NIST's "Control-P" function of managing data at fine granularity ([FTC guide via Manatt summary](https://manatt.com/insights/articles/2016/new-ftc-guide-offers-tips-for-protecting-personal); [NIST Privacy Framework core](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-privacy-framework-version-1-core-pdf)). The FTC guide was read only through summaries and its 2023 PDF listing, so treat the exact wording as secondhand. **Make the audience the gate and the roster share the channel**: an answer reaches a team only when a share to that team exists and the question names that team, and dana and private notes are never on the list. **Offer "as counts only" as an audience** for logistics: Retreat Guru's kitchen Meal Report gives daily totals per meal type with an asterisk for diet notes ([Retreat Guru: meals and meal reporting](https://help.retreat.guru/knowledge/meals-and-meal-reporting)), and Planning Center's Overview report totals dropdown and checkbox answers ([Planning Center: reports](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139346-create-reports-on-attendees.html)), so a kitchen or housekeeping team can plan without seeing names. Counts need structured answers, so the dietary question should be choices (vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, allergy with a write-in) rather than free text. A severe allergy is the honest exception: the kitchen may need a name at the serving table, so that question should say so.

**Tell the registrant who reads each answer, in one line under the question, generated from the audience setting** ("Read by the registration team and your teachers"). GOV.UK's service manual says to "make sure you know why you're asking every question" and to explain what happens to personal information in brief help text of about three lines, and to allow "I'm not sure" where it is a valid answer ([GOV.UK: designing good questions](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/designing-good-questions)); the W3C forms tutorial adds "ask only for data the task needs" ([W3C WAI: forms tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/)). **Make sensitive questions optional with "prefer not to say"**, and ask accessibility needs as one open, optional question with its audience stated. Finally, **an audience may be narrowed at any time but never silently widened** for answers already given, since those people answered under a narrower promise; widening should apply only to new answers or ask the registrant again. That last rule is an inference from the purpose-limitation principle, not a documented product behaviour.

One prerequisite comes first. RIM stores answers by the question's wording, so renaming a question orphans its answers and its audience. Tito gives each question a fixed "export name" that becomes its CSV column and keeps questions in a library attached per ticket type ([Tito: questions](https://go.tito.io/docs/events/create-a-question)). **Per-field audience, counts for the kitchen, a retention rule and safe exports all need answers tied to a stable question identity.** No product documents what happens when questions change after people answer; the safe pattern is to keep each answer bound to the version of the question it answered.

### Sensitive data: HIPAA does not apply, but care and deletion do

For a nonprofit that is not a health plan, clearinghouse or billing provider, **HIPAA does not apply**; the Privacy Rule "applies only to covered entities" ([HHS: covered entity decision tool](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/emergency-preparedness/source-not-covered-entity/index.html)). A nonprofit law firm advises such organizations not to call themselves "HIPAA-compliant" ([Wagenmaker & Oberly](https://www.wagenmakerlaw.com/blog/hipaa-and-medical-records-for-nonprofits), secondary source summarized from search). Wisconsin's breach statute requires reasonable efforts to notify people after unauthorized acquisition of personal information ([Wis. Stat. 134.98](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/134/98/3/a)); whether health answers fall inside its definition was not confirmed. The FTC's five steps (take stock, scale down, lock it, pitch it, plan ahead) ask organizations not to collect sensitive data without a need and to keep it "only as long as it's necessary" under a written retention policy. GDPR is not binding on RIM but is a useful reference: data revealing **religious belief** is a special category, even when inferred "with a reasonable degree of certainty" ([LegalVision: special category data](https://legalvision.co.uk/data-privacy-it/special-category-data/); [ICO policy document](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/our-information/safeguards-policy/policy-document-our-processing-of-special-categories-of-personal-data-and-criminal-offence-data/)). Registering at a Buddhist centre can itself imply belief, so **even a name on a roster is sensitive**, which makes count-only a real default rather than a lesser option.

The practical rules: justify every health, grief or trauma question by who acts on the answer; clear sensitive answers automatically a set number of days after the program, recording "answers cleared" in history while keeping the fact of registration and attendance; publish a short plain-language retention page; keep export a Registration-team power, log each export, and exclude sensitive answers by default. Tito keeps export away from its Staff role and sends check-in devices "only the reference by default", so a lost phone exposes nothing ([Tito: check-in day](https://pro.tito.io/docs/guides/check-in-day)); RIM's equivalent is that notification emails carry links, not answers.

### History and notes are the team's shared memory

**Only the enterprise tools keep a full per-registration history.** Certain has a History tab listing each change with date, user, action and summary, and lets planners add their own entries (search summary; the page returned 403) ([Certain: registration history](https://platform-support.certain.com/hc/en-us/articles/30521115960855)). Rock keeps an Audit Log of how a registration was entered and its financial changes ([Rock: Manage Event Registrations](https://community.rockrms.com/documentation/church-management/event-calendar/event-registrations/manage-event-registrations)), Tito a per-ticket activity log ([Tito: attendees](https://go.tito.io/docs/events/attendees)), and Retreat Guru a Changes Log (vendor marketing). Planning Center has no edit history: it relies on rich-text Notes visible only to staff, a communications tab that keeps emails for at least three months with failed deliveries in red, and an instruction to email the registrant yourself after an edit ([Planning Center: edit attendee information](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139338-edit-attendee-information.html); [Planning Center: communication history](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/188711-view-communication-history-in-registrations.html)). For a small team of rotating volunteers, history plus authored notes (the Certain model) is the strongest pattern. It should record creation (by the person, by a registrar on their behalf), status changes, dana events, every email sent, answer edits (who and when, not the old sensitive value), check-ins, and shares and exports. Notes need an audience too, defaulting to the Registration team, since a note about grief is as sensitive as the answer that prompted it.

### Check-in is a minimal-data role, and RIM's Zoom door is already an attendance signal

Every product treats the door as **a separate role that sees the least**: Tito Door, Eventbrite's check-in roles (which "must not have any additional roles or permissions"), Humanitix scanning-only access, and Planning Center's headcounter. Tito's check-in shows "still to arrive", undoes mistakes, filters lists per door, and queues check-ins offline ([Tito: check-in](https://go.tito.io/docs/events/check-in); [Tito: check-in day](https://pro.tito.io/docs/guides/check-in-day)). Planning Center's roster stations show only complete registrations, flag balances or missing forms before check-in, and let an unregistered person be checked in as a guest ([Planning Center: take attendance](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/138315-take-attendance-for-registrations-signups.html)). Multi-session courses need **per-session attendance**: Arlo records it per session, while Wild Apricot's check-in is event-level only (both search summaries) ([Arlo: session attendance](https://support.arlo.co/hc/en-gb/articles/214079446-Record-and-report-on-attendance-for-individual-sessions); [Wild Apricot: attendance](https://gethelp.wildapricot.com/en/articles/34-event-attendance)).

For online sessions, Humanitix's Virtual Event Hub **checks the attendee in automatically when they open their unique link** ([Humanitix: virtual event hub](https://help.humanitix.com/en/articles/8896988-event-guide-deliver-your-online-event-with-the-virtual-event-hub)), which is the same shape as RIM's Zoom door: a registrant passes through RIM to reach Zoom, so RIM can record "entered through RIM" for that session. Arlo's alternative, a per-registrant Zoom join URL with attendance recorded by Zoom, needs Zoom registration switched on and a unique email per person ([Arlo: Zoom integration](https://support.arlo.co/hc/en-gb/articles/900000750923-Zoom-integration-overview), search summary), which conflicts with RIM's decision not to use Zoom registration. Zoom's own participant reports list join, leave and duration, but someone who rejoins appears as several rows and a waiting room can distort join times ([Cornell IT: Zoom reports](https://it.cornell.edu/zoom/use-zoom-reports)), so they make a secondary check at most. In person, a greeter needs names and a large check-in control at phone width, any answer explicitly marked for greeters (such as step-free entry), a way to add a walk-in for the registrar to finish later, and nothing else. Open drop-in sits can use a headcount with no names.

## Waitlists, cancellations and money: where care and convenience meet

### Waitlist offers should hold a place, expire kindly, and pass on

Waitlist automation splits three ways. **Manual release** is the norm in church tools and some ticketing: Planning Center either promotes one person or emails the whole waitlist, first come first served; Rock promotes manually; Eventbrite releases with a response window but does not move on to the next person when it lapses ([Planning Center: waitlist](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139349-waitlist.html); [Eventbrite: release tickets to the waitlist](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/817355/)). **Notification without a held place** creates a race: Humanitix emails people every 30 minutes but does not reserve the ticket, and it has a help article for people who accepted an offer only to find it sold out ([Humanitix: waitlist](https://help.humanitix.com/en/articles/8892176-enable-and-manage-a-waitlist-for-your-event); [Humanitix: offer but sold out](https://help.humanitix.com/en/articles/12821601-i-received-an-offer-of-tickets-through-the-waitlist-but-the-tickets-are-now-sold-out), title only). **Sequential offers with a hold** remove both the registrar's chore and the scramble: in Tito's current product "each offer holds the place for a chosen time", an unanswered offer returns the person to their old position, offers can be withdrawn, and while anyone waits, freed places stay off the public page ([Tito: waitlist](https://go.tito.io/docs/events/waitlist)); CiviCRM moves the next person to "Pending from waitlist" with a set expiry in hours before offering the next ([CiviCRM: online event registration](https://docs.civicrm.org/user/en/latest/events/online-event-registration/)); Arlo does the same (search summary). Momence's "fastest fingers first" texts everyone and lets the first reply win (search snippet, [Momence: waitlist FAQs](https://help.momence.com/en/articles/12028307-waitlist-faqs-classes)). For a sangha, the held, expiring, one-at-a-time offer is the caring choice. When a program fills for good, people still waiting should hear so gently; Tito Classic's silent removal ("will not be notified") is the opposite ([Tito Classic: waiting lists](https://help.tito.io/en/articles/2003015-waiting-lists)).

### Cancelling: tell the person, and keep money a separate step

**Cancel and refund are separate actions everywhere**, and no product refunds automatically on cancellation ([Planning Center: cancel or restore](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139339-cancel-or-restore-registrations-and-attendees.html); [Planning Center: refunds](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139369-refund-payments.html)). Products that send a notice when the organizer cancels include Eventbrite, which lists cancellation notices for organizer-cancelled orders among its automatic emails, Retreat Guru's automated cancellation email, and CiviCRM's status-change emails on moves to Cancelled ([Eventbrite: automatic emails](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/748624/); [Retreat Guru: emails](https://help.retreat.guru/knowledge/emails-v2); [CiviCRM: keeping track of participants](https://docs.civicrm.org/user/en/latest/events/keeping-track-of-events-and-participants/)). RIM today notifies the registrar and not the person, which leaves someone who believes they are coming. A gentle cancellation notice, with a choice to suppress it for cases like a duplicate, closes that gap, and Planning Center's practice of leaving post-edit emails to staff memory argues for a prompt after any staff change: "Let them know about this change?"

On **registrant self-cancel**, reported as information only: it is widespread but almost always a switch the organizer turns on, often off by default and limited by a cutoff. Planning Center has a "Congregants can cancel their own registrations" toggle that cancels the whole registration and leaves refunds to a request ([Planning Center: signup settings](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139323-change-signup-settings.html)); CiviCRM adds a cancel-or-transfer link to the confirmation that closes a set number of hours before the start, and acts only on the lead booker ([CiviCRM: online event registration](https://docs.civicrm.org/user/en/latest/events/online-event-registration/); [CRM-19002](https://issues.civicrm.org/jira/browse/CRM-19002)); Humanitix's self-refund is off by default ([Humanitix: buyer journey](https://help.humanitix.com/en/articles/13548552-the-ticket-buyer-journey-on-humanitix)); Ticket Tailor can keep donations when an order is cancelled and refund to a voucher ([Ticket Tailor: self-serve](https://help.tickettailor.com/en/articles/8975333-how-can-i-enable-self-serve-order-management-for-my-ticket-buyers)). The documented pitfall is Arlo, whose self-cancel neither touches the refund nor alerts staff (search summary, [Arlo: registration policies](https://support.arlo.co/hc/en-gb/articles/115005325123-Registration-policies-self-cancellation-self-transfers-and-course-terms-and-conditions)). Where self-cancel exists, a freed place feeding an automatic waitlist closes the loop without a registrar. RIM's current choice stands; this is the landscape it sits in.

### Dana, tiers and support requests

**No product has a first-class "no one turned away" workflow**; in the field it is a $0-floor donation price for low-cost offerings plus a scholarship rate with human review for costly residential retreats. Spirit Rock publishes five named rates, offers a scholarship rate through a separate Google Form, and says about 20% of participants receive one; teacher dana is invited at the end of the retreat, separate from the fee ([Spirit Rock: Labor Day 2026](https://www.spiritrock.org/programs/retreats/356r26-labor-day-insight-retreat)). IMS lets people choose a scholarship tier during registration by answering "some simple questions" ([IMS: fees](https://www.dharma.org/retreats/retreat-center/fees/)). Retreat Guru's sliding scale can carry a required minimum, a direct model for RIM's base plus dana. CiviCRM's approval statuses ("Awaiting approval", then a link to finish within a set number of hours) show how a held "support requested" state could work inside the app rather than in a Google Form. One cheap insight comes from Tito, the only product that reports how many paid below, at and above the suggested amount and the average ([Tito: pay what you want](https://go.tito.io/docs/events/pay-what-you-want)); for dana, that shows whether the suggestion is set kindly. Ticket Tailor charges no platform fee on donations ([Ticket Tailor: donations](https://help.tickettailor.com/en/articles/950984-how-to-accept-donations-for-your-event)), consistent with RIM's no-fee stance.

### Registering others, households and transfers

Every major product lets staff add a registration by hand. Planning Center picks or creates each person, can override capacity, and asks whether to send the confirmation ([Planning Center: register people](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/139336-register-people-for-a-signup.html)); Humanitix and Eventbrite record complimentary, cash or bank payments without charging a card ([Humanitix: manual order](https://help.humanitix.com/en/articles/8896997-create-complimentary-tickets-and-offline-sales-with-a-manual-order); [Eventbrite: add attendees](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/874192/how-to-add-attendees-manually/)). Wild Apricot's staff-added registrants do not get a confirmation unless staff press "Send notification" (search summary), a pitfall to avoid. For households, Planning Center, Rock, Tito and CiviCRM all separate the person who submits from the people attending; Rock deliberately starts its family dropdown blank so the submitter must choose who is coming ([Rock: registration templates](https://community.rockrms.com/documentation/church-management/event-calendar/event-registrations/registration-templates)). Transfers come in two kinds: to another person (Tito reassigns by changing the email and lets organizers lock changes near the start) and to another date (Rock's Move Registration, Ticket Tailor's self-reschedule) ([Tito: reassign](https://help.tito.io/en/articles/3586025-how-do-i-re-assign-my-ticket), search summary; [Tito: move an attendee](https://go.tito.io/docs/events/move-an-attendee-to-another-ticket-type)). A transfer to another person should clear the original person's answers, since the new person answers for themselves.

## The build list: three phases and a short list to leave out

The order follows two principles from the evidence. Memory and privacy come first because they are foundations other features lean on, and because sharing rosters without them would expose sensitive answers. Then come the tools that make each program run with less registrar effort and more care. Large data-model changes (households, refunds) come last, but one of them, several people per registration, reshapes the registration record, so decide whether it is coming before the history schema hardens.

### First: memory, privacy and care

| # | Build | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stable question identity**: answers tied to a question record, not its wording; answers bound to the version they answered | Tito's fixed export names; every later item (audience, counts, retention, exports) depends on it |
| 2 | **Per-field audience** with Registration-only default, "as counts only" option, optional plus "prefer not to say" for sensitive questions, a one-line "who reads this" under each question, and no silent widening | Retreat Guru's per-question role permissions (only precedent); GOV.UK, W3C, FTC need-to-know; no product shows the audience to the registrant |
| 3 | **History timeline plus authored notes** on each registration (author, date, note audience), including every email sent | Certain, Rock audit log, Tito activity log, Planning Center notes and communications tab |
| 4 | **Roster sharing** by audience (team or teachers) and level (count, names, names and contact, plus answers the field allows), per program, no re-sharing, an access panel listing who sees what, and every share logged | Tito scoped roles, Humanitix restricted access, Planning Center managers and Counter, Dhanakosa |
| 5 | **Export as a separate Registration-team power**, logged, sensitive answers excluded by default | Tito keeps export from Staff; Eventbrite bundles answers into basic reports (the risk to avoid) |
| 6 | **Register someone on their behalf** (phone, paper, walk-in), source recorded, "send confirmation" choice defaulting to yes, capacity override | Planning Center, Rock, Eventbrite, Humanitix; Wild Apricot's no-notification pitfall |
| 7 | **Cancellation notice to the person**, plus a "let them know about this change?" prompt after staff edits | Eventbrite, Retreat Guru, CiviCRM notices; Planning Center leaves it manual |

### Next: running each program well

| # | Build | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | **Reminders counted back from the start** (default the day before), optional fixed-date override, a short "starting soon" note with the room link for Zoom programs, per occurrence for recurring programs, keeping "everyone who hasn't had it" | Eventbrite 48 h, 2 h, 10 min online; Wild Apricot, Planning Center, Realm relative timing; CiviCRM reaches late registrants |
| 9 | **Message all or a filtered group** from Registration, with preview and test send, factual and kept on the transactional mail path rather than Flodesk; the Programs editor offers "send an update to N people" when time, place or messages change | Eventbrite and Tito preview and test; Postmark's separate streams; Humanitix keeps reminders non-promotional |
| 10 | **Waitlist offers one at a time** that hold the place, expire, and pass on; added or freed places go to the waitlist first; a gentle note when the waitlist closes | Tito, CiviCRM, Arlo; the Humanitix race; Tito Classic's silent removal as the counterexample |
| 11 | **Attendance per session**: "entered through RIM" for Zoom, a greeter door view at phone width with walk-ins, a headcount for open sits; attendance used for care, never penalties | Humanitix hub auto check-in, Tito Door, Planning Center roster stations and headcounts, Arlo session attendance |
| 12 | **Logistics reports**: kitchen counts from structured dietary answers, a rides and housing list for the teams named on those questions, a small library of standard RIM questions carrying preset audiences | Retreat Guru Meal Report and housekeeping list; Planning Center overview totals; Tito question library |

| 15 | **Dana distribution view**: how many gave below, at and above the suggestion | Tito's pay-what-you-want report |

### Later: when a real need appears

| # | Build | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | **Several people in one registration** (submitter plus attendees, each with their own answers; family list starts blank) | Planning Center, Rock, Tito, CiviCRM |
| 17 | **Transfers** to another person (answers cleared) or another date (dana carried, history recorded), locked near the start | Tito reassign and lock, Rock Move Registration, Ticket Tailor reschedule |
| 18 | **Support request** for programs with a required minimum: a held state the registrar answers, replacing outside forms | Spirit Rock Google Form, IMS questions, CiviCRM approval statuses |
| 19 | **Refunds inside the app** through Stripe, always a step after cancelling, never automatic | Planning Center, Rock refunds by method and reason |

### Leave out: in tension with dana and care

| Leave out | Why |
|---|---|
| Late-cancel and no-show fees, session deductions | Mindbody and Momence charge them; they turn absence into debt, against "turns no one away" |
| Discount codes, early-bird and dynamic or scarcity pricing | Dana already lets each person choose; urgency pricing works against it |
| Card-fee pass-through and resale | RIM never charges fees; resale treats a place as a commodity |
| "Fastest fingers" or whole-waitlist broadcasts | They create a scramble; held one-at-a-time offers serve people in order |
| Silent waitlist removal | Leaves people waiting without word |
| Per-registrant Zoom registrant links | Need Zoom registration, which RIM avoids for its rate-limit pitfall; the RIM door already gives attendance |
| Auto-charging instalment plans on a stored card | Heavy machinery for a dana model; a registrar conversation covers the rare need |
| Wallet passes, badges, QR kiosks | Built for crowds; a greeter list fits a sangha |
| Claiming "HIPAA-compliant" | HIPAA does not apply; say plainly who sees what and how long it is kept |

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the question. "Where do confirmations and reminders live?" turns out to have a settled answer across the market, words with the program and sending with the people, so the two-app split RIM has chosen is not an invention; it is how Rock, Planning Center and Eventbrite already divide the work, and RIM's existing site-wide-template-plus-program-message design is the sturdier of the patterns found. The more consequential finding is that the market offers almost no guidance on the thing Jesse cares about most: who may read an answer about grief, health or belief. Only Retreat Guru restricts answers by role, nobody tells the person at the question, and nobody clears those answers afterwards. For RIM that is an opening rather than a risk, provided the plumbing comes first: stable question identity, a Registration-only default, a visible "who reads this", and a rule that deletes what is no longer needed.

The second shift is in what "missing" means. RIM is not behind on selling features, and most of the market's depth (tiers, codes, fees, resale) would make RIM worse. What RIM lacks is the registrar's memory and the person's assurance: who changed this and why, what we told them, whether they know they were cancelled, whether the offer will wait for them. Those are small to build compared with households or refunds, and they are the features a rotating volunteer Registration team will lean on every week.
