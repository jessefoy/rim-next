# Programs Page — Balanced Audience Review

## Why the prior page felt wrong

The prior version was attractive, but it behaved too much like a first-visit landing page:

- the largest supporting block told everyone to start with a drop-in;
- multiple colored cards gave every program similar visual importance;
- category, badge, description, schedule, location, and action repeated at the same scale;
- color changed from card to card without a dependable relationship to program type;
- experienced practitioners, community members, and people seeking sustained study had to scroll past newcomer framing before recognizing themselves.

The issue was not simply density. It was **audience imbalance and undifferentiated emphasis**.

## Revised content role

The Programs page is now a crossroads for people at different stages of relationship with RIM. It does not ask visitors to identify as a persona. Instead, it presents several valid modes of participation:

1. weekly meditation and Dharma teaching;
2. daily practice;
3. sustained study and deepening;
4. community, movement, spiritual friendship, and peer support;
5. retreats and special programs.

Newcomer guidance remains available, but it is one quick path alongside the current schedule and member access rather than the page's dominant message.

## Persona coverage

- **Health and Healing Seeker:** compassionate, non-performative descriptions; Recovery Dharma and heart practice are visible without framing all programs as treatment.
- **Mindful Living Practitioner:** schedule, format, and commitment remain visible without opening a detail page.
- **Cautious Newcomer:** a first-visit route and an open-to-all weekly gathering are easy to find, but do not dominate the directory.
- **Depth Seeker:** Dharma study, teachings, sustained practice, and retreat are elevated into distinct sections.
- **Sangha Steward:** community, Kalyana Mitta, movement, and peer support are presented as central practice rather than secondary extras.
- **Engaged Bodhisattva:** generosity and community pathways are visible contextually, while service remains primarily the role of Community and Support pages.
- **Existing members:** member access appears as a first-class utility path rather than only in the global navigation.

## Visual system

- Program rows use one calm, neutral surface instead of individually saturated cards.
- Color belongs to a program family, not to an individual item. It appears only in a small symbol, label, and subtle featured tint.
- Category names and plain-language descriptions carry meaning; color is never required to understand the structure.
- The page uses a compact title scale and more restrained section spacing than the earlier card directory.
- Program rows are fully clickable, while the explicit “Program details” label supports less technical visitors.
- No filters, dropdowns, or hidden interactions are required.

## Relationship to the rest of the site

- **New Here** owns detailed reassurance and first-visit expectations.
- **This Week** owns current dates and immediate attendance decisions.
- **Programs** explains the range and rhythm of practice over time.
- **Teachings** demonstrates Dharma depth through freely offered resources.
- **Community** communicates belonging, spiritual friendship, and service.
- **Member Area** provides links and tools for people already participating.

This division prevents the Programs page from repeating the entire website.

## CMS behavior

Each program row supports:

- program type;
- title;
- short description;
- schedule;
- format or location;
- commitment language;
- destination URL.

Groups are CMS collections organized by a category or program-family reference. Optional related links, such as the teachings library, should disappear when their field is empty. Program rows do not depend on identical title or description lengths.

## Content status

Program names and schedules in this prototype are representative of the current design work. Production content should be populated from the live CMS and reviewed before publishing. Kalyana Mitta is included to demonstrate how spiritual-friendship offerings can appear within the program architecture; its final schedule and availability should come from the CMS.
