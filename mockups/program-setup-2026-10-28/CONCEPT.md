# Setting up a program, step by step

A mockup for Jesse's review, 2026-10-28. **Not built.** Open `index.html` in a browser and use the bar at the top. Nothing here ships. The live stylesheet supplies the look; the words are provisional until Jesse reads them aloud.

## Why

The RIM Guide's review, *Scheduling in RIM: a volunteer's-eye review* (2026-10-28), found that the program editor lets a volunteer set up a schedule that doesn't mean what they think, and never shows them the session dates. Checking it against the code found more. "Number of occurrences" gives one session too few when the start day isn't ticked, and one too many when two days are ticked and the number is even. Weekly with no day ticked means every day. Calendar files run from the start to the End date as typed.

Jesse asked for a setup that walks people through without errors, lets them go back and change their mind, and is the same for the whole program, not only its dates.

## The idea in five parts

1. **One question at a time**, in the order a person decides: About, When and where, Teachers and teams, How people join, Messages to those who register, Dana, Where it appears. The seven sections people already know.
2. **The trail.** Answers build up beside the question, or above it on a phone. Tapping any answer goes back to it. Changing your mind keeps everything that still fits. Only what no longer fits is asked again, and the screen says why.
3. **A draft until Publish.** It's saved as you go, so stopping halfway loses nothing. No one sees it and no team is told until Publish.
4. **The review.** Every answer is shown as a sentence, each section with its own Change. What publishing will set off is said before it happens. Publish stays off while something it needs is missing, and each missing thing links to its question.
5. **After it's set up**, the program's page is those same sentences. Change opens only that section's questions. Before a change is saved, it says what it does: sessions that go, volunteers who are told, a rotation that ends, an update for the people registered.

## The schedule, made so it can't go wrong

- **How often:** Once · Several days in a row · Every week · Every other week · Once a month. These are choices, not a number box, so "every 20 weeks" can't happen.
- **Days:** for a weekly program, the days. For a monthly one, which one of the month (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th or last) and which day. Today the monthly pattern is worked out from the start date, where no one can see it.
- **Time:** from and to, with the length always shown ("45 minutes"). A long one gets a plain question: "That's 13 hours. Is that right?"
- **First session:** a calendar where only the days it meets can be chosen, from today on. A first session that isn't a session can't be picked, so Save never has to refuse one.
- **Ends:** It goes on · After a number of sessions · On a date (again, only real session days). The sessions are listed as they're set: "Fridays, 7 to 7:45 PM. 4 sessions: Fri Oct 30, Nov 6, Nov 13, Nov 20."
- **End Date & Time is gone for a repeating program.** A one-time program asks for its day; several days in a row ask for the first and last day and the times they begin and end.

The rule behind the list is the one the rest of the site would use: the first session is always a real session, "after N" counts real sessions, and "on a date" includes that day.

## Jesse's rulings (2026-10-28)

1. **Daily, and "every N" beyond every other week: left out.** How often is Once · Several days in a row · Every week · Every other week · Once a month.
2. **"Show on My Home from a date": only when asked.** Under "Where should people find it?", a quiet "Only from a date…" opens the calendar.
3. **A true draft: yes.** A program in progress is saved as you go, emails no team, and appears nowhere until Publish.
4. **The order of programs: by when they meet, with "Show it first" for priority.** Jesse asked where Sort order applies ("you can't see it in relationship to the other programs") and how to prioritize when programs show in different places. Answer below.
5. **The words** are provisional until Jesse's read-aloud.

## The order of programs

Order matters only in the lists where people browse: each section of Programs & Events, and the Kalyana Mitta community groups page (the same Community Groups). This Week and My Home are calendars and stay in time order. A section has one order, so it reads the same everywhere it shows.

The order, with no numbers to manage:
- **Programs shown first**, in the order chosen ("Show it first in its section", in "Where should people find it?"). When more than one is shown first, the preview offers Move up and Move down for those.
- **Then programs that go on**, by their first day of the week (Monday first, as on This Week) and time.
- **Then programs with an end** (once, several days, or a series with an end), by their first session.

The same step shows the section as visitors will see it, with this program in its place. A new program always lands in the right place, and taking away "first" puts it back where its time belongs. The live page keeps its order, except Our Hearts Were Made for This (Sunday 9:00) moves ahead of Recovery Dharma (Sunday 9:30). The Programs list gets a page showing every section's order.

## What building it would involve

- The schedule rule, shared by the editor, the RIM Guide, This Week, My Home, the Scheduler, reminders and calendar files: exact counts, the first session always a real session, an "ends on" date.
- The draft state and Publish.
- The walk-through and the program's page of sentences, replacing the one long editor page. The questions live in one place, so the RIM Guide asks the same ones in conversation.
- "Before you save" for changes to a published program, starting with the schedule.
- The Scheduler's words from the review (team, not hub; plain words for "sub-cover" and "self-assigned"; no em-dashes; errors that sound like a person), and, as the review suggests, "Reset this team" kept for admins (your call).
- The "Setting up a program" tutorial and the guide's Programs topic, rewritten to match.
